Grammar notes and declension tables

Verbs that take the dative — special verbs

These verbs take a dative as their primary object, even though English translates them with a direct object:

persuadeo + dat.persuade — tibi persuadeo
resisto + dat.resist — hostibus resisto
studeo + dat.study/be eager for
credo + dat.trust/believe
occurro + dat.meet
impero + dat.order
placeo + dat.please
succurro + dat.help, run to help
invideo + dat.envy
Dative indirect object — "give/send/tell X to someone"

These verbs take a dative indirect object for the person and an accusative direct object for the thing:

do + acc. + dat.give X to someone — donum puellae do
mitto + acc. + dat.send X to someone — epistulam regi mitto
narro + acc. + dat.tell X to someone — fabulam pueris narro
dico + acc. + dat.say X to someone — verba militi dico
trado + acc. + dat.hand over X to someone — gladium duci trado
reddo + acc. + dat.give back X to someone
ostendo + acc. + dat.show X to someone
scribo + acc. + dat.write X to someone
respondeo + dat.reply to someone
ad + accusative — motion toward a place

Use ad + accusative (not dative) for motion toward a place or thing:

ad urbem eoI go to the city
ad portam currithe runs to the gate
ad insulam navigathe sails to the island
ad castra contenduntthey march to the camp

Key distinction: "give the sword to the soldier" → dative (militi). "Go to the city" → ad + acc. (ad urbem). The test: is "to" expressing receiving (dative) or destination (ad + acc.)?

Irregular imperatives: dic, duc, fac, fer

Four common verbs have shortened singular imperatives:

dico → dicsay! (not dice)
duco → duclead! (not duce)
facio → facdo/make! (not face)
fero → fercarry/bring! (not fere)

The plural imperatives are regular: dicite, ducite, facite, ferte. Remember: ferte (not ferite).

Imperatives of sum, nolo — and verbs with none
sum → es, estebe! (to one / to several)
adsum → ades, adestebe present!
absum → abes, abestebe absent!
nolo → noli, nolitedon't!, refuse! (used with an infinitive: noli currere = don't run)

volo and possum have no imperative form (you can't order someone to “want” or “be able”).

Present imperative formation
1st conjugationsg: porta · pl: portate
2nd conjugationsg: mone · pl: monete
3rd conjugationsg: rege · pl: regite
3rd -io conj.sg: cape · pl: capite
4th conjugationsg: audi · pl: audite

The singular imperative is just the present stem. The plural adds -te (or -ite for 3rd/4th).

Time expressions — no preposition needed

Latin expresses time without prepositions. The case alone tells you the meaning:

Accusative = duration"for how long"
tres annosfor three years
multos diesfor many days
Ablative = when / within"at what time" or "within"
primo annoin the first year
tribus diebuswithin three days
tertio dieon the third day

Never use in or per for time in Latin. The accusative/ablative case alone does the job.

qui, quae, quod — relative pronoun rules

The relative pronoun introduces a subordinate clause. Two rules determine its form:

Gender & numberagree with the antecedent (the noun it refers to)
Casedetermined by the relative pronoun's role within its own clause

Examples:

puella quam videothe girl whom I see (f sg — agrees with puella; acc — object of video)
miles qui pugnatthe soldier who fights (m sg — agrees with miles; nom — subject of pugnat)
rex cuius filium amothe king whose son I love (m sg — agrees with rex; gen — "of whom")
-io verbs (3rd conjugation)

These 3rd conjugation verbs end in -io and follow a slightly different pattern — their stem vowel is i throughout the present tense:

3rd declension i-stems (gen. pl. -ium)

Some 3rd declension nouns take -ium in the genitive plural instead of -um. There are three groups:

1. Monosyllabic stems ending in two consonants

urbs, urbis f.city → urbium
mors, mortis f.death → mortium
nox, noctis f.night → noctium
mons, montis m.mountain → montium
pons, pontis m.bridge → pontium
dens, dentis m.tooth → dentium
parens, parentis m.parent → parentium

2. Same nominative and genitive singular (-is, -is)

hostis, hostis m.enemy → hostium
civis, civis m.citizen → civium
navis, navis f.ship → navium
classis, classis f.fleet → classium
mensis, mensis m.month → mensium
collis, collis m.hill → collium
feles, felis f.cat → felium (same number of syllables in nom. and gen.)

Exceptions — same pattern but not i-stems (gen. pl. -um):

canis, canis m.dog → canum
iuvenis, iuvenis m.young man → iuvenum

3. Neuter i-stems — also change abl. sg. to -ī and nom/acc pl. to -ia:

mare, maris n.sea — abl. marī, pl. maria, marium
moenia, moenium n.city walls (plural only)
Genitive plural: -um or -ium?

Most 3rd declension nouns take -um. Only i-stems take -ium. Quick test, in order:

Neuter in -e, -al, -ar→ -ium (mare → marium)
Nom. and gen. sg. have the same number of syllables, ending -is / -es→ -ium (civis → civium, navis → navium, feles → felium) — except canis → canum, iuvenis → iuvenum
One syllable, stem ends in two consonants→ -ium (urbs → urbium, mons → montium, nox → noctium)
Everything else→ -um

Typical -um nouns (not i-stems):

rex, regis → regumlex → legum · dux → ducum · vox → vocum · pax → pacum · lux → lucum · pes → pedum · flos → florum
more syllables in the genitivemiles → militum · eques → equitum · comes → comitum · custos → custodum · obses → obsidum · senex → senum · hiems → hiemum
pater, mater, frater→ patrum · matrum · fratrum
-or, -o, -tas, -tusamor → amorum · leo → leonum · legio → legionum · virgo → virginum · libertas → libertatum · virtus → virtutum
neuters in -en, -us, -utnomen → nominum · tempus → temporum · corpus → corporum · caput → capitum
caudex, caudicis→ caudicum

Adjectives: all 3rd declension adjectives take -ium (fortis → fortium, ingens → ingentium, felix → felicium). Some i-stem nouns have an alternative -um form too (parentum, mensum, felum); the trainer accepts either.

Reflexive pronouns

The reflexive refers back to the subject. It has no nominative and is the same for all genders, singular and plural:

acc.se
gen.sui
dat.sibi

Example: rex se laudat — "the king praises himself". Compare with rex eum laudat — "the king praises him" (someone else).

Reflexive se vs emphatic ipse: se is the object of the verb and points back to the subject (rex se laudat — the king praises himself). ipse only adds emphasis to a noun and agrees with it in case (rex ipse venit — the king himself came). In the quizzes, se is shown as “(reflexive)” and ipse as “(emphatic)” on the prompt to tell them apart; when you answer in English just “himself” (etc.) is enough.

User Guide

Getting started

Choose a direction (English → Latin or Latin → English), select a quiz type, tick the categories you want to practise, and press Begin. The trainer pulls words from every ticked subcategory and shuffles them into a quiz. First time here? The ⚙ Settings → Help has a basic tour and an advanced tour.

Quiz types

Vocabulary tests word meanings. Noun / Adjective / Pronoun declension drills case forms. Verb conjugation drills person/number forms in the tenses you choose. Sentences generates full Latin or English sentences to translate, with five difficulty levels.

Answer modes & scoring

Typed — type your answer and press Enter or click Check. Multiple choice — click one of four options (or press 1–4). Correct = 1 point. Press Tab for a hint: if you then answer correctly you get ½ point. Skip (Escape) = 0 points. Matching (vocabulary only) — click an English word and its Latin partner from two columns of five. Matched words vanish and are replaced in the same slots by halves of different pairs, so partners turn up later; nothing reshuffles. Matching on the first try scores a point; a wrong click marks the word for review.

Typing tips & tags

When typing a Latin verb you can add dat or acc to the end to show the case it takes (credo dat, porto acc); after a preposition add abl or acc. Anything in (brackets) in an English meaning is optional. When two words share a meaning, an extra tag on the prompt (like “1st declension” or “starts with a”) tells you which word is wanted. For 3rd-declension nouns give nominative, genitive and gender. For words listed with several forms (hic haec hoc, ille illa illud, qui quae quod, idem/eadem) you can type just one of the forms — any one counts. If you make a small slip in spelling (for example ancills instead of ancilla), you get one free retry with no penalty — but a genuine wrong ending or wrong word still counts as wrong.

Unfinished quizzes

If you leave a quiz part-way (with Menu, or by closing the page), the trainer remembers exactly where you were. Next time you open it you'll be asked “Finish a quiz?”, and an Unfinished quizzes list stays at the top of the Practice tab until you finish or discard each one. The last 5 unfinished quizzes are kept (starting a sixth drops the oldest). Sentences aren't resumed. Questions you already answered are saved and are not asked again; your score carries on and the whole quiz is counted as one session.

Spaced repetition

Every word tracks how often you've seen it and your accuracy. Words you get wrong come back sooner; words you know well are spaced further apart. The “Due” button starts a session with overdue words first.

Mark as hard (★ Starred)

Tap the ☆ in the top-left of a question card to flag that word as hard. You can also star words in the word list and in the forms popup. Press ★ Starred on the setup screen to practise only those words in the selected quiz type (for the declension and conjugation drills, only starred words of that type are used). Tap the star again to remove a word.

Word list & search

The Word list tab on the right edge opens a drawer showing your selected words with their spaced-repetition stats. Type in the search box to look through every word by Latin or English — results show its category and any daily box it belongs to. The ★ Starred button in the drawer lists only your hard words.

Seeing a word's forms

Click any word in the drawer to open its forms: a case table for nouns and adjectives, a present / imperfect / future table plus imperatives for verbs, and the full paradigm for pronouns. During a quiz the Forms button on the card does the same — after you've answered (or any time in Latin → English). Press Escape to close.

Speed mode

Toggle speed mode in the start bar. A countdown timer appears — if it hits zero, your current typed text is auto-submitted (correct if right, wrong if wrong). Empty input counts as a skip. For sentences, the timer goes up to 60 seconds.

Verb tenses

In verb conjugation mode, checkboxes let you choose any mix of Present (porto, portas, portat), Imperfect (portabam, portabas), Future (portabo, portabis) and Imperative (porta, portate). The irregular verbs sum, eo, volo, nolo, fero and others are covered.

Sentences

Sentence mode builds a random sentence for you to translate. Subjects can be I, you, we, you (pl.) (shown only by the verb ending, so ego/tu/nos/vos are optional), or nouns in the singular or plural; sentences also use the genitive (filius regis), dative (pueris dona damus) and vocative (Servi, portate cibum!). “You” on its own means one person; “you (pl.)” means more than one. Tick the tenses you want: Present (miles gladium portat), Past (portabat — “was carrying”), Future (portabit — “will carry”) and Commands (porta gladium!; plural commands add “all of you”). The difficulty levels move from simple sentences to prepositions, adverbs and time phrases, then clauses joined by quod/sed, relative clauses, and finally three- and four-clause compounds. Hover or tap a word in the sentence for its meaning.

Sentences — eius vs suus

Sentences distinguish possessives: [own] means reflexive (suus/sua/suum — refers back to the subject). [someone else's] means eius (indeclinable — refers to a different person).

Daily practice

Date-stamped boxes at the bottom of the Practice tab contain curated word sets for specific lessons, grouped by month. Tick a month or individual date collections, expand one to choose word types, and press “Practice these” for a focused quiz. Every daily word is also in the main categories.

Word packs

Under Daily Practice there is a Word Packs box for themed word sets (not tied to a date). Tick one or more packs and press Practice, or tick categories too and press Start to practise them all together. Current packs: Colours, Time & calendar.

Custom words

Click “Custom words” above the category grid to add your own vocabulary. Choose a category, enter the Latin and English, and the word is added to the relevant subcategory. Custom words are saved and can be removed individually.

Statistics

The Stats tab shows total answers, accuracy percentage, day streak, a recent-session chart, and per-category breakdowns with due-word counts. Your stats are saved automatically in this browser, including sessions you leave early. “Reset all data” clears them.

Export & import progress

Your progress lives in this browser only (clearing site data or switching device loses it). At the bottom of the Stats tab, Export progress downloads a backup file containing your statistics, spaced-repetition data, custom words, starred words and theme. Copy as text does the same to the clipboard. To restore, press Import progress and pick the file (or paste text), then choose Replace (overwrite everything) or Merge (combine, keeping whichever record has more practice).

Information tab

Reference tables for all grammar: noun declensions (1st–5th), adjective declensions, verb conjugations (present, imperfect, future, imperative), pronoun paradigms, number declensions, and special cases (dative verbs, time expressions, relative pronoun rules, cum+pronoun forms). Wide tables scroll sideways on narrow screens.

Settings

The ⚙ Settings button above the Practice / Stats / Information tabs holds everything you can adjust: how long to wait after a right or wrong answer, typo forgiveness, sound effects, theme, text size, reduced animations, showing the leaderboard & chat, rank style, and the Help menu (tours, this guide and keyboard shortcuts).

Example sentences

Click any word in the word list, or press Forms during a quiz, to see its forms and a set of example sentences that use it, each with an English translation. Hover over (or tap) any underlined Latin word to see which form it is and why, e.g. puellis: feminine ablative plural because in takes the ablative.

Reading: stories & parsing

The Reading tab has short stories: translate one sentence at a time, then compare with the model translation and mark yourself (hover any word for help). Parse it highlights a word in a sentence and asks for its form (e.g. dative plural or present, 3rd person plural), then explains why. Correct parses earn leaderboard points.

Daily goal, streak & mistake review

Set a daily goal in Settings; the Practice page shows today’s progress and your 🔥 streak of days in a row you reached it. The Stats tab has a mistake review of the questions you miss most, with a button to practise them.

Challenges, reactions & your look

Click someone on the leaderboard and press ⚔ Challenge: pick which words can come up, and you both answer the same 10 questions; the higher score wins (a tie goes to whoever was faster). Your challenges are under ⚔ Challenges beside your name. React to chat messages with 👍 😂 ❤️ 😮, and press Look to choose an avatar and name colour. Use the search box to find anyone on the leaderboard, with their real place.

Leaderboard & chat

Join with a first name or nickname to appear on the leaderboard (one point per correct answer, and for a sentence, one point per Latin word (Latin → English) or the number of English words × 1.2, rounded down (English → Latin): today, this week, this month and all time; a short quiz of 5 questions or fewer stops earning points from the 3rd time you repeat it in a day) and to use the class chat. Your points show as ⭐ during a quiz. Changing your name updates it everywhere, including old messages.

Themes

Open ⚙ Settings and choose Change theme… to open the theme picker. Choose from 11 themes: Dark, Light, Parchment, Mist (light themes), Midnight, Forest, Rose, Ocean, Violet, Slate, and Sepia (dark themes). Your choice is remembered.
